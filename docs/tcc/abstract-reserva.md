#! ABSTRACT

Food inflation weighs especially on low-income families, and comparing prices across supermarkets is an effective way to save money, but checking several websites or stores manually takes time and effort. This work presents SuperPreço, a web application that gathers the prices of four supermarkets in Poços de Caldas, Minas Gerais, Brazil, and shows where each product, and the whole shopping list, is cheaper. Prices are collected automatically twice a day by a robot that opens the supermarkets' websites in a browser and reads the information displayed, a technique known as web scraping. The system standardizes product names to recognize the same item on different websites and calculates the price per kilogram or liter, allowing packages of different sizes to be compared. The platform was built with React.js, Node.js, Express.js, MongoDB and Selenium WebDriver and deployed on free hosting services. About 27,000 products were monitored. Only 34.7% of them appear identically in more than one supermarket, a share that rises to 72.7% when similar products from other brands are considered. Among the products sold in more than one supermarket, the median difference between the highest and the lowest price was 19.3%, and the complete basic food basket cost up to R$ 167.32 more per month depending on the supermarket. In a manual check, the identification of the same product was correct in 88% of the cases. The results indicate that no supermarket is the cheapest for everything, which reinforces the usefulness of the tool.

**Keywords:** Price Comparison; Supermarkets; Web Scraping; Web Application.
